Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Charlotte Homeowners Should Know

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. It could come from an upstairs bathroom, plumbing inside the ceiling, an AC condensate line or the roof. Nothing is repaired until the origin of the water has been tracked down.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. Our crew removes no more ceiling than necessary, takes out soaked insulation, dries the framing and the drywall that stays, and finishes with a patch, texture and paint that match. If the ceiling has started to droop, treat it differently. Do not stand beneath it, and get help on the way.

Warning Signs

  • A smoke detector, register or fixture with water running from it
  • Drip marks at the top of a wall where it meets the ceiling
  • Joint tape coming loose, with screws and panel edges telegraphing through
  • Tan or rust colored circles that widen following a storm or a shower
  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • Stale, mildew like odor you cannot trace to anything you can see
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Spreads Above

Water runs along joists and soaks into insulation. Because the panel soaks from its top side, nothing may look wrong from underneath.

Hours 24 to 48

Marks Start Showing

A stain forms and paint starts to blister. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Days 3 through 7

Sagging Sets In

The panel turns soft and starts slipping off its fasteners. Between the joists the surface dips, joints split apart and a musty smell arrives below.

After a week

Sections May Fall

Waterlogged sections can fall, bringing wet insulation with them. The repair zone is far bigger at this stage, with mold well rooted inside the cavity.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

Damage on the Floor Above

Whatever is over the ceiling, a subfloor or attic decking, has typically been soaked as well. Our inspection includes the space overhead, since moisture left there keeps feeding the problem.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Water adds a lot of weight to a panel while weakening its hold on the framing. A bulge can drop a whole section onto whatever is underneath.

Growth on the Hidden Side

The top side of the drywall and the insulation resting on it stay wet long after the surface feels dry, which is where mold tends to grow unseen.

Our Process

Our Ceiling Water Damage Repair Process

1

Trace the Leak

Using thermal imaging and moisture meters, our team follows the water to its origin and inspects the attic or room overhead. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Make It Safe

Furnishings are protected or relocated, and electricity to affected lights is turned off. A ceiling with pooled water is relieved carefully, with the water collected as it comes out.

3

Remove and Dry Out

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. Air movers and a dehumidifier dry the joists and surrounding ceiling, and we apply antimicrobial where the water was not clean.

4

Repair and Finish

When everything reads dry, our crew replaces the insulation, hangs drywall, finishes the seams, blends the texture with the rest of the ceiling, then applies stain blocker and paint.
