Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Charlotte, Charlotte Restoration Co answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

Sewage damage is classified as Category 3 (black water), the most contaminated and dangerous category of water damage. This classification requires the most stringent cleanup protocols, including full personal protective equipment, containment barriers, removal of all porous materials that contacted sewage and thorough disinfection of every remaining surface. For Category 3 water damage the reference is IICRC S500, and our crews follow it to bring the building back to safe occupancy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

How Our Sewage Cleanup Works

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

1

Safety Assessment

First the crew identifies hazards, determines how much of the property the sewage has contaminated and puts containment in place to protect rooms that were not affected.

Why Professional Sewage Cleanup Matters

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Strong sewage or rotten egg odors coming from drains or lower levels
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line
  • More than one drain in the house overflowing or backing up together

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Route sump pump and gutter water away from the sewer line, which can back up when storm flow overloads it
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
