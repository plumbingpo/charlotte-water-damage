What Usually Goes Wrong in Durham Homes

Every house has a few weak points where water tends to get loose. These are the ones Durham property owners report to us again and again.

Water heaters: Sediment collects in the bottom of a tank and slowly eats through the steel. The first sign is often a rusty puddle or a damp ring on the floor around the base. A split tank does not stop at one tankful, because the inlet keeps feeding it until the valve is shut. When the unit lives in an attic or interior closet, the water reaches carpets and ceilings far from it.

Roofs: Wind can lift shingles and drive rain underneath them in a single storm. Skylights, plumbing vents and chimneys are other entry points once their seals crack with age. Since water follows the framing downhill, the ceiling stain rarely sits directly below the actual opening. For roof leaks in Durham, we tarp where needed, pull out soaked insulation and dry the structure so the ceiling can be repaired.

Freezing: Cold nights, depending on the season, can break plumbing that showed no sign of trouble earlier. The first to go are hose bibs, irrigation lines and any pipe routed through an unheated space. Vacant homes and rooms with the heat turned down are especially exposed. If a faucet only trickles on a freezing morning, shut off the water and call before the pipe thaws.

Backups: A drain that gurgles or several fixtures that slow down together point to a blockage in the main line. Once it closes off fully, anything you flush or drain returns at the lowest opening in the house. Since the waste is hazardous, we extract it and then sanitize concrete, tile and other hard surfaces. Until a plumber opens the line, do not use sinks or toilets and keep everyone clear of the mess.

Seepage: Dampness at the base of a basement or foundation wall often means water is collecting outside. The usual reasons are grading that tilts inward, clogged perimeter drains and downspouts that empty too close. White chalky deposits, peeling masonry paint and a persistent damp smell are early warnings. The team at Charlotte Restoration Co can extract standing water and dry the space, and we will tell you what we think let it in.

How Durham Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. For homeowners in Durham, prevention is mainly a matter of knowing where to look.

Plumbing comes first. A water bill that climbs for no reason, or pressure that seems too high, can signal a concealed leak or strain on the system. Appliances are second. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. Third is the outside of the building. Rain gets in through damaged roofing, failed flashing, clogged gutters and ground that tilts toward the walls.

Leaving the house empty for a while? Close the main valve before you go, or have a neighbor look in. Homeowners in Durham can ask Charlotte Restoration Co about any of this. If something does fail, calling quickly is what keeps the damage contained.
