Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Charlotte

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. We pump the space dry, remove the ruined material and get the wood dried while it is still sound.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. We also look for where the water came from, a leaking supply line, a drain, or groundwater after rain, so it does not fill back up.

What It Looks Like

  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Ducts and plumbing lines below the house covered in condensation
  • Rust on ductwork straps, pipe hangers or metal support posts
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
What We Do

How We Handle Crawl Space Water Removal

1

Safety Check and Inspection

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

Submersible pumps and extraction hoses remove the standing water, including the low spots and footing trenches where it collects. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Exposed joists and subfloor are cleaned and treated with an antimicrobial where growth has started or is likely.

4

Final Drying and Checks

A dehumidifier and low profile air movers stay running beneath the floor until the lumber meters at a normal level. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Weakened Floor Framing

Rot often starts on top of the joist or at the sill plate, where you cannot see it from the hatch. Checking with a probe is how we catch sections that have gone soft.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Termites and Other Pests

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Getting moisture levels down takes away a main reason for them to stay.

Water That Is Not Clean

Water under a house is often not clean. Groundwater full of soil and waste, or a drain leak, counts as Category 2 or 3, which calls for protective equipment and disinfecting.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Water collects in the low spots and soaks into the soil. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Mold Gets Started

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Day 3 to 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.
